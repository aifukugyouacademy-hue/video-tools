How it works

Two different things can happen when frame rates differ. Conform (slow down or speed up): the same frames are played at the new rate. The duration becomes duration × source fps ÷ target fps, and audio changes speed and pitch unless it is also corrected. 24 → 25 fps (the classic PAL speed-up) makes a film about 4.17% shorter; 23.976 → 24 makes it 0.1% shorter. Convert (keep the real duration): frames are repeated or dropped (or blended) so the length stays the same. The number of frames becomes duration × target fps.

Frame counts use the exact rates (23.976 = 24000/1001, 29.97 = 30000/1001, 59.94 = 60000/1001).

Quick start

Choose the source and target frame rates and type the source duration. You get the frame count, the conformed duration, the speed change and the frame count after a duration-preserving conversion.
