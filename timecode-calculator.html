How it works

Timecode counts frames in the form hours : minutes : seconds : frames. At a whole frame rate such as 24, 25 or 30 it is simple: frames = ((hours × 3600) + (minutes × 60) + seconds) × fps + frames. For example 00:10:00:00 at 25 fps is 600 s × 25 = 15,000 frames.

Why 23.976, 29.97 and 59.94 are different. These rates are the whole rates slowed by a factor of 1000/1001 (29.97 is really 30000/1001 = 29.970 fps). Timecode still counts 30 frames per “second”, so non-drop timecode slowly runs behind the wall clock, about 3.6 seconds per hour at 29.97. Drop-frame timecode fixes this by skipping the frame numbers 00 and 01 at the start of every minute except every tenth minute (at 59.94, four numbers are skipped). No picture is dropped; only the labels. Drop-frame timecode is shown with a semicolon before the frames, like 00:10:00;00.

23.976 fps has no drop-frame version; it is counted like 24 fps. The “real time” line below the result is the frame count divided by the true frame rate (for example 30000/1001), so you can compare timecode with the clock.

When you subtract and the answer would be negative, the calculator shows the result with a minus sign.

Quick start

Choose the frame rate, then type a timecode or a frame count. In the Add / subtract tab, enter two timecodes and choose + or −.
