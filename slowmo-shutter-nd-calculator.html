How it works

Slow motion. If you shoot at 120 fps and play on a 24 fps timeline, each second of action takes 120 ÷ 24 = 5 seconds on screen, so playback speed is 24 ÷ 120 = 20%. Use a shooting rate that is a whole multiple of the timeline rate when you can; 23.976 ⇄ 24 differences are usually too small to notice.

180-degree shutter rule. Shutter speed = 1 ÷ (fps × 360 ÷ angle). At 24 fps and 180° that is 1/48 second, which gives natural-looking motion blur. A smaller angle (for example 90°, 1/96) looks sharper and choppier; a larger angle blurs more. Cameras usually offer the nearest speed, such as 1/50 for 24 fps.

ND filters. One stop halves the light. ND factor = 2 to the power of stops (ND8 = 3 stops, ND64 = 6 stops, ND1000 = about 10 stops). To keep your shutter speed at 180° outdoors, add ND instead of speeding up the shutter.

Quick start

Pick a tab. In Slow motion, enter the shooting and timeline frame rates. In Shutter, enter the frame rate and angle (or a shutter speed). In ND filter, enter the stops or the factor.
