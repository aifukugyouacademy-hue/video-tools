How to use it

Subtitles appear too late? Enter a negative shift, for example -1.5 moves every line 1.5 seconds earlier. Too early? Use a positive number. Lines that would start before 0:00 are clamped to 0:00 and counted in the status line.

Subtitles start in sync but drift further off over time? That is a frame-rate mismatch, not an offset. European (PAL) releases often play 23.976 fps film at 25 fps, which makes the video about 4% shorter, so subtitles made for the 23.976 fps version fall further behind with every minute. Pick the line that describes your files; every timestamp is multiplied by the ratio (for example × 23.976 ÷ 25), and the shift is applied after that.

SRT or VTT? SRT uses a comma before milliseconds (00:01:02,500) and numbered blocks. WebVTT starts with WEBVTT, uses a dot (00:01:02.500) and is what the HTML5 <track> element and many web players expect. YouTube, Vimeo, DaVinci Resolve and Premiere Pro import both. When converting VTT to SRT, cue settings such as align:start and the header notes are dropped because SRT has no place for them.

Quick start

Paste your subtitles or open a file, set the shift in seconds or choose a frame-rate fix, pick SRT or WebVTT, then download or copy the result.
