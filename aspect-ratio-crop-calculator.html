Turn a 16:9 shot into a 9:16 Short, a 4:5 feed post or a 2.39:1 widescreen frame. Get the largest crop that fits, where it sits, and the exact size to export. Nothing is uploaded.

How it works

To crop to a new ratio, the calculator keeps as much of the frame as possible. If the target is narrower than the source (for example 9:16 from 16:9), the full height is kept and the width is cut: crop width = source height × target W ÷ target H. If the target is wider, the full width is kept and the height is cut instead. The offset tells you where the crop starts, measured from the left or top edge, which is what the crop or position fields in DaVinci Resolve, Premiere Pro and FFmpeg expect.

Sizes are rounded to even numbers because H.264 and H.265 with 4:2:0 color need even width and height. That rounding can shift the ratio by a fraction of a percent, which no viewer will notice.

FFmpeg users can paste the command shown under the result. It crops first, then scales, so the export keeps full detail from the source.
